🇺🇸 Can Americans Retire in Croatia?

Yes — Americans can legally retire in Croatia on a Temporary Stay permit for retirees (dozvola privremenog boravka). You apply at the Croatian embassy in the US before moving. The key requirements are:

  • Proof of stable income of at least ~$1,200/month (pension, Social Security, investment income, or equivalent savings)
  • Comprehensive private health insurance valid in Croatia
  • Proof of accommodation (rental agreement or property deed)
  • Valid US passport + clean FBI background check (apostilled)

The permit is initially issued for 1 year and is renewable. After 5 continuous years of legal residence, you can apply for permanent residency. Citizenship is possible after 8 years.

Note: Croatia joined the Schengen Area in 2023, meaning your Croatian residence permit also allows travel across most of Europe.

🇬🇧 Retiring to Croatia from the UK

Since Brexit, UK citizens are treated as non-EU nationals and follow the same process as Americans. You need a Temporary Stay permit, applied for at the Croatian consulate in the UK. Requirements are identical: proof of income (~€1,000–€1,500/month), private health insurance, accommodation proof, and a clean DBS/ACRO criminal record check.

The good news: Croatia's cost of living is significantly lower than the UK, English is widely spoken in tourist areas, and flights from the UK to Split or Zagreb are frequent and affordable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Americans retire in Croatia?

Yes. Americans retire in Croatia on a Temporary Stay permit, requiring ~$1,200/month in provable income, private health insurance, and a clean background check. Apply at the Croatian embassy before travelling. After 5 years you can apply for permanent residency.

How to retire in Croatia from the UK?

UK citizens (post-Brexit) apply for the same Temporary Stay permit as non-EU nationals at the Croatian consulate in the UK. You'll need proof of €1,000–€1,500/month income, private health insurance valid in Croatia, an accommodation agreement, and an apostilled criminal record check.

How much money do you need to retire in Croatia?

A couple can live comfortably on $2,000–$2,500/month all-in. Split costs around $1,760/month, Zagreb around $1,900/month. The visa minimum income requirement is $1,200/month, but $2,000+ allows a genuinely comfortable lifestyle including dining out regularly.

What is the Croatia retirement visa requirement?

Croatia's Temporary Stay for Retirees requires: (1) ~$1,200/month stable income, (2) private health insurance valid in Croatia, (3) proof of accommodation, (4) valid passport and clean criminal record. The visa is applied for at a Croatian embassy or consulate in your home country.

What is the cheapest city to retire in Croatia?

Split is the most affordable major retirement city at ~$1,760/month for a couple. Zagreb is slightly higher at ~$1,900/month but offers better hospital access. Dubrovnik is the most expensive at ~$2,200/month but has the best climate and scenery.

Is there inheritance or income tax in Croatia?

Inheritance tax is 4%, capital gains tax 10%, dividend tax 10%, and there is no wealth tax. Foreign pension income may be exempt from Croatian tax under applicable tax treaties — consult a Croatian tax advisor for your specific situation.
